Last week I shared where RedlineIQ stood after three prompt iterations.

Then I ran a clean test, and it failed with complete confidence.

I'd marked up a sheet with 13 redlines myself. It returned 12 findings. None of them were mine.

Miss a markup and it's annoying. Invent 12 at high confidence and it's dangerous.

I assumed it was the prompt, so I told it not to fabricate and to flag anything it couldn't read. It kept inventing. One run returned "BEAM NEEDS TO BE INSTALLED BEFORE CEILING JOISTS" four times, text nowhere on the sheet. Not the prompt.

It was resolution. Sonnet caps image input at 1568 px on the long edge. A 36×24 sheet with four plan views reaches the model at around 43 DPI. My callouts fell below what it could resolve, so it did what models do with an unreadable image: it filled the gap with statistically plausible notes.

It wasn't lying. It couldn't see.

The fix wasn't a better prompt. I split each sheet into 15 overlapping tiles, each under the 1568 px ceiling, ran extraction per tile, then merged. Same model, same prompt. Recall jumped to 0.85, 11 of 13, and I shipped it to the live demo that night.

The takeaway: before you tune a prompt, check what the model can actually see. Most of my "prompt problems" were input problems.

But tiling wasn't free: to surface those 11 real marks it flagged 29, dropping precision to 0.38. Then Ilija Mirkovic, in the comments on my launch post, named what I'd been missing: over half my inputs are digital PDFs, and the markup is already structured text in the file, coordinates and all. I was running vision OCR on data I could have just parsed. So the next build is a hybrid: parse the digital markup straight from the file, and save tiled vision for the scanned and hand-drawn sheets that actually need it. That should bring precision back without giving up the recall, toward full coverage.

When a false positive costs more than a miss, how do you tune that tradeoff?
